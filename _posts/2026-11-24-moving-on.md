So.  We’re moving on.  Or, more precisely, we’ve moved on, because now it’s Tuesday afternoon.  We’ve had a couple of nights here in Chamarel, already.  This is inland, in the south-west of the island, some 260 metres up, effectively in a forest.  Or is that ‘jungle’?  Whatever, there are trees with huge leaves, birds with vivid-coloured feathers (clashing with the car) and roads with precipitous drops and often little paving.  We’re glad to have a car with good ground clearance.    

![car beside house in Chamarel]({{"/assets/images/car_by_bush_house.png"|relative_url}})

We’ve also come to realise that Mauritius is not so big.  In fact, with a car, just about anywhere is accessible to anywhere else within 90 minutes or so.  Once you’ve slowly crawled over the track that barely passes as a road between our (new) accommodation - which is amazing - and the centre of Chamarel.  There are only a small handful of properties nearby, so meeting another car coming in the opposite direction is both rare and challenging.  Fortunately, when it has happened, they have chosen to hive off into a ditch, rather than us! 

![on the less well-made roads]({{"/assets/images/car_meets_dog.png"|relative_url}})

So, whilst there’s plenty to see and do here, it’s also been possible for us to visit another coastline with its dramatic scenery.  In fact, we’ve been able to get sufficiently high up to see the sea from where we are, beyond the mountains.  
  
![the view from breakfast]({{"/assets/images/chamarel_to_morne.png"|relative_url}})  
  
This scene is from our breakfast location yesterday morning, with both (a very tiny) me and a large basaltic monolith in the far distance, called Le Morne Brabant, more about which, later.  Oh, and you can see the sea, too, if you squint.
