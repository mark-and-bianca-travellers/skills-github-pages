We’ve become “regulars” at both the Bamboo Bar and Naked Foods, the first in the evening and the last each morning. Breakfast at Naked has been lovely and fascinating, for the food and the company. Each morning we’ve found ourselves talking with someone from a different country or culture (or both).  

On Monday we met Abraham, a young man from Israel who had been gravelling around Africa for several months, in between stints in the Israeli military.  His World-view was fascinating, not least because of his dislike of Benjamin Netanyahu - which he felt was common with most of his countrymen - but also his total commitment to the actions of his country in Gaza and the West Bank.  He’d travelled in many countries, noting that it was often uncomfortable for others when he let people know he was Israeli. We asked about his view on the film Naza, and the people who made it.  All lies from unpatriotic people.  But that they could make such a film was proof of the free country that Israel is.  It was eye-opening to listen to what he had to say.  
  
Tuesday, with Bianca and I at the same table, Abraham’s seat was filled by a German lady, on the last day of her solo 3-week vacation touring Mauritius and Reunion.  Though language was a slight barrier - her English was very good, our German lousy - we talked about places to go and places not to.  It’s amazing how many people we meet can speak at least 2 languages well, and often 3 or more.  Bianca’s thinking of learning some French, which would be quite useful for our Interrail trip.  
  
This morning’s breakfast was in the company of the owner of the Bamboo Bar, his wife and baby son, who happened to be eating in Naked.  She’s from Mauritius and he’s Australian.  They met there some years ago and returned to Mauritius to raise a family.  They bought the bar, and the rest is history.

![Bamboo Bar tonight]({{"/assets/images/bar_tonight.png"|relative_url}})
  
Tonight, in Bamboo, over a marvellous meal, we sat next to a table of young, Arabic-speaking men and women, who turned out to be an Emirates air crew.  A combination of Algerians, Egyptians, Israelis and Moroccans, much of their conversation was in English which, they said, they could better express themselves in.  Who knew?

![Bianca and the Emirates crew]({{"/assets/images/bianca_with_aircrew.png"|relative_url}})
  
Not sure what we’re up to tomorrow, but we have a catamaran booked for Friday 😁
