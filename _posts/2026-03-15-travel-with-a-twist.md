Today we said goodbye to the Santa Maria resort, returned the “special features” car and began our extended journey to Eleuthera, via a night in Nassau.
  
All packed and ready to go by 7am, we walked the boardwalk for the last time to the club house, where we ordered and ate overly large breakfasts and drank bottomless cups of coffee, gazing out at the beach and sea.  By 8:30am we had checked-out and jumped into the car for the fifteen-or-so minute drive to the airport.  Presley, our ever-helpful rental guy, was ready to meet us in the car park.  

![Presley and Mark at the airport]({{"/assets/images/airport.png"|relative_url}})

We walked for perhaps 30 meters to the airport terminal, a collection of low-rise buildings which would accommodate twenty people at most.  Check-in, where our luggage was weighed on a simple scale, was in a building large enough for 3 passengers at best.  After our tags were attached, we headed to customs; another building where a single passenger group was allowed to enter, one at a time.  Each of our bags was opened and inspected, whilst we walked through the usual metal detector, installed in the middle of the room.  

![Stella Maris airport terminal]({{"/assets/images/stella_maris_airport.png"|relative_url}})

Sufficiently inspected, we were instructed to place our bags with a collection of others, before being directed to the departure area.  This was a covered space with seats for twenty or so, next to the wire fence separating passengers from the runway.

![Stella Maris departure lounge]({{"/assets/images/departures.png"|relative_url}})
  
News rustled through the waiting passengers that someone’s taxi driver had heard that there had been a bomb threat at Nassau airport (our destination) and that had resulted in the airport being closed.  Frantic pawing over various websites showed that this was indeed true, and the plane that was to be taking us, was still on the ground, in Nassau.  

![Notice of bomb threat]({{"/assets/images/threat.png"|relative_url}})

We waited.  There are worse places to wait.  Sitting in the open but shaded departure area, with blue skies and 27°C, watching wealthy people arrive in their private jets from who-knows where, all having to enjoy the wonders of this very basic aerodrome.  
  
In the shop that sells aviation fuel for $11 / gallon, were also Foxes Glacier Mints at 15 per $, together with Snickers bars and Bounties.  Heaven!  We bought supplies (no fuel) and sat back down to continue waiting.  I’d found enough websites to tell every possible combination of our inbound flight being both late and cancelled, our outbound flight being late and / or cancelled and the inbound flight having arrived, though it clearly hadn’t.  It was all very confusing, as there are no flight information screens at all - well, nothing electronic, anywhere!  The only positive information was the site of our baggage on a trolley, sitting at the side of the runway.  

![Luggage on the runway]({{"/assets/images/luggage.png"|relative_url}})

Eventually, after a couple of hours waiting, I resorted to the FlightRadar24 site, to see the inbound flight just ten minutes away from arriving.  Phew!  Once it had, it passengers disembarked and its hold emptied of bags, we marched around the edge of the runway to wait at the bottom of the steps.  Whilst we had assigned seats, we were told where to sit once we got on - essentially just the rows over the wings - there were not sufficient people to fill the plane. 

![Waiting at the steps to board]({{"/assets/images/boarding.png"|relative_url}})
  
Forty minutes later we were disembarking (deplaning?) at Nassau, wondering over the runway apron, keeping clear of the other aircraft loading and manoeuvring about.  

![Walking between the planes]({{"/assets/images/on_the_apron.png"|relative_url}})

After quickly collecting our bags we dived into Wendy’s in the terminal for lunch (we were not expecting to be in a position to eat again, today) before venturing outside to find the hotel shuttle bus.  Nassau is very busy, especially when compared with Stella Maris, with dozens of taxis and coaches and hundreds of people milling about.  Eventually we discovered that the hotel shuttle doesn’t ‘shuttle’ at all - there isn’t one!  We thought about walking - the hotel is less than a mile from the airport - but there isn’t a sidewalk, and a taxi was asking over $30 for the ten minute trip.  We called the hotel.  “No problem”, they said, “we’ll collect you”.  A few minutes later a very smart Mercedes arrived and we were whisked off to the hotel.  What a service!  
  
A few minutes later, we were checked-in, bags dropped in our room, and walking to a sea-front bar for a well-earned beer.  Or two.  

![A bottle of beer]({{"/assets/images/beer.png"|relative_url}})
