title: "Sometimes it rains, even in the Bahamas!"  
date:  2026-03-18 19:36:07 -0400  
---  
Well.  Here we are in the deepest, darkest Bahamas, and it’s raining!  I assumed that has to happen sometimes, and here we are to witness it.  It’s not rain like at home, of course.  This is rain that you don’t mind walking through because, whilst we’ve been out in it, it hasn’t been heavy, and it has been warm.  Warm rain.  We did get fairly soaked in the rain in Nassau the other day, but that was just a brief shower in the morning.  Today, and by the looks of the forecast for the next few days, it’s going to be raining all day long.  No worries; there are plenty of chairs to hang wet clothes over as they dry, and it hasn’t stopped us getting out and about.  
  
This morning, as previously advertised, we visited Spanish Wells.  There was a brief moment of concern as the car decided that it didn’t want to start, but with Bianca pressing the secret combination on the key fob, all was well.  Spanish Wells is a little island off the northern end of Eleuthera, requiring a sixteen mile drive to Gene’s Bay Dock to catch a small ferry boat over to the island.  

![Spanish Wells ferry boat ]({{"/assets/images/spanish_ferry.png"|relative_url}})

Obviously, we are not the only people wanting to take the ferry (which runs each 15 minutes or so) making parking an exciting prospect.  We arrived at about 9:30am, to a full and overflowing carpark, with the sides of the road leading up to it also filled with parked vehicles.  We parked in a bush, some fifty meters from where the ferry was waiting.  Helped aboard by a welcoming captain (pilot?) we took our seats and paid our $24 for the two-way trip, carefully storing away our two casino chip-like tokens for the return journey.  It doesn’t take too long to make the crossing (10 - 15 minutes), and we and the handful of other passengers were soon disembarking on Spanish Wells.    
  
The island is quite lovely.  Lots of narrow roads with brightly coloured wooden buildings; multitudes of golf carts whisking people about; many fancy boats in the extensive harbour area.  We chose a less common route away from the harbour to find a place for breakfast.  

![Spanish Wells street scene]({{"/assets/images/spanish_wells.png"|relative_url}})

Unfortunately, the cell signal strength on the island wasn’t brilliant, so navigation soon came down to asking a local-looking person (he was washing a drive way) where we should go.  The Wreckers Café was the answer to that and, after another ten minutes of walking, we were there.  A perfect location, overlooking the sea and marina (and a giant “Trump 2028 Make America Great” flag), offering an excellent menu of breakfast delights.  Along with the usual non-stop coffee, we sat and ate, discussing the World’s problems, which we have now fully resolved.  Unfortunately, during this critical conversation, it began to rain.  Not too much to be concerned about, but enough to feel that an additional Espresso Martini was necessary, to save us from walking in the drizzle.

![Bianca with espresso martini]({{"/assets/images/espresso_martini.png"|relative_url}})

Eventually, feeling a little uncomfortable occupying a table that others were clearly lining up to take, we chose to wander back through the little town, enduring the gentle downpour, to take the ferry back to Eleuthera.  
  
One of the experiences we were least expecting on our travels was the significant use of cash.  Not really a surprise when you think about it; sat on one of a group of islands in the Caribbean Sea, where power often fails and banking is “difficult”, not everywhere is able or willing to take payment by card (especially American Express, that “doesn’t do nicely” quite often).  So, it’s not unexpected that we were joined on the ferry by a guy carrying a large sack of money, escorted by two others armed with shotguns!  All three were in bullet-proof vests.  Waiting at the dock was an armoured truck, ready to take them away.  Not something I’ve seen before at such close quarters.

![view from the ferry, leaving Spanish Wells]({{"/assets/images/leaving_spanish_wells.png"|relative_url}})
  
Given it was still softly raining, we made our way back to our accommodation, via another dock, where the ferry to Harbour Island departs.  The overall situation and parking looked much the same as we’d just experienced, preparing us for another trip later in the week.
